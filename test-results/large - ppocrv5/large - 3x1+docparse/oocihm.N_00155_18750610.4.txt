JUDGMENT OF PRESIDENT DRAPER.
In the interval between the adjournment of
the Court yesterday evening and the meeting
this morningI carefully read and considered
the whole evidence. The result at which I
arrived in regard to the acts of the respond-
ent and others on the polling day, and during
the hours appointed for taking the polls at
Davidsons Hotel, in the village of Carlisle,
rendered it unnecessary in my opinion to de-
termine any other of the charges advanced
for the purpose of avoiding the election. My
finding and my report to the Speaker will be
limited to that one matter.
It will be convenient to begin by referring
to the statutory provisions on which this
charge of corrupt practices is founded. They
are contained in the Ontario statutes,
32 Vic., ch.21sec.66 ； 36 Vic. ch.2, sec.1
and sec. 3sub-sec.1 and 2.
The first of these enactments is," Every
hotel, tavern, and shop in which spirituous
or fermented liquors or drinks are ordinarily
sold shall be closed during the day appointed
for polling in the wards or municipalities in
which the polls are held; and no spirituous
or fermented liquors or drinks shall be sold
or given to any person within the limits of
such municipality during the said period,
under a penalty of $1oo, in every such case."
2nd. "' Corrupt practices or corrupt
practice ' shall mean bribery, treating, and
undue infnenice, or any of such offences as
defined by this or any act of the Legislature,
or recognized by the Common Law of the
Parliament of England; also, any violation of
the 46th, 61st, or 7lst sections of the Elee-
tion Law of 1868, and any violation of the
66th section of such last mentioned Act dur-
ing the hours appointed for polling.
3rd:" When it is found upon the report
of a judge upon an election petition that any
eorrupt practice has been committed by any
candidate atan election, or by his agent,
whether with or without the actual know-
ledge and consent of such candidate, the
election of such candidate if he has been
elected, shall be void. And further, when
it has in like manner been found " that any
corrupt practice has been committed by or
with the actual knowledge or query, if this
should not be and) consent of any candidate
at an election in addition to his election, if

during the eight years next after the date of
his being so found guilty be incapable of be-
ing elected," & c., & c.
It will be seen, therefore, that the first
provision above stated prohibits certain
things, and subjects the persons who act

$100 in every such ease. The second, among

LIGHTHOUSE ILLUMINATION.— ThE NECSS
of intense illumination for lighthouses has
induced the English Government to under-
take various experiments with a view to
realize this object. At one of their coast
stations there are employed for this purpose
two magneto-electric machines each eing
put in motion by a two-horse power steam
engine, and, excepting. wear and tear, the
whole consumption of material to produce
the light is the coke and water required to
raise steam for the engine and carbon points
for the lamp in the lantern. The lamp is a
delicate arrangement of machinery, hold
ing the two carbons between which the
electric light exists, and regulating their ad-
justment, so that while they gradually con-
sume away the place of the light shall not
bealtered. The electrie wires end in the two
bars of a small railway, and upon these the
lamp stands. When the carbons of a lamp
are nearly gone that lamp is lifted off and
another instantly pushed into its place. The
light never goes out through any deficiency
or cause in the engine and machine house,
and when it has become extinguished in the
lantern a single touch of the keepers hand
sets it shining as bright as ever, sending
forth its illumination with a power of bril-
liancy far surpassing that of any other fixed
light.

A sad accident happened one mile from
Blairton, on the 3lst ult., by which William
MeArthur, an old resident of Marmora, lost
his life. The deceased who lived on the
shore of Marmora Lake, had occasion to go
down the lake during the day on returning
in the evening, when within a few feet of the
shore, the boat upset, throwing Mr. MeAr-
thur into the water, and he was drowned..
The body was recovered during the night.
Walter and Fred Roe, aged nineteen and
fifteen, sons of the Postmaster at Newmarket,
left early on Monday morning for the Hol-
land River for the purpose of fishing, and
were to have returned home last evening, but
up to this time no tidings of them can be
heard. They hired a boat at Holland Land-
ing and went down the river to the bay on
Lake Simcoe. Parties were out all night,
and to-day several have gone in search. A
tug has been engaged at Bell Ewart to search
the bay. Some hopes are entertained of
their having drifted to some Island, but
most grave doubts exist regarding their
safety.
